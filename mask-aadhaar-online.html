How to Mask Your Aadhaar Number Online (Safe, UIDAI-style Masked Aadhaar for KYC)

Updated July 2026 · 6 min read · KaagazPDF Guides

Handing over a full Aadhaar copy for every small KYC is risky — your 12-digit number can be misused if it lands in the wrong hands. A masked Aadhaar fixes this neatly: it hides the first 8 digits and shows only the last 4, so you stay verifiable without exposing your complete number. This guide explains what a masked Aadhaar is, when it is accepted, and how to mask any Aadhaar photo, scan, or PDF for free — right on your own phone or computer, with nothing uploaded to any server.

What is a masked Aadhaar?

A masked Aadhaar is simply your Aadhaar with most of the number hidden. Instead of showing all 12 digits, it displays only the last 4 digits — for example, XXXX XXXX 1234. Your name, photo, and those last four digits stay visible, which is usually enough for a business to match your identity against their records, while the sensitive part of the number remains private.

The idea comes from UIDAI's own "masked Aadhaar" option, but the concept applies to any copy you share. Whether it is a printout, a photo sitting in your gallery, or a PDF, the goal is the same: reveal just enough to prove who you are, and hide the rest.

Why is a masked Aadhaar safer to share?

Your full Aadhaar number, together with a photo of the card, can be misused for fraudulent verification or quietly linked to services without your knowledge. Once you hand over a full copy — at a hotel, a mobile store, or a coaching centre — you have no control over where that scan travels next.

A masked copy cuts this risk sharply:

  • Less exposure: Only 4 digits are visible, so the copy is far less useful to anyone who should not have it.
  • Still verifiable: Many banks, telecom operators, and other services accept a masked Aadhaar for KYC where full disclosure is not strictly required.
  • Peace of mind for photocopies: If a masked print is left behind on a shop counter, the harm it can do is limited.
Tip: Even on a masked copy, it helps to write the purpose and date across it (self-attestation style), e.g. "For SIM KYC only — 25/07/2026". A clear purpose line discourages anyone from reusing the copy elsewhere.

When should you share Aadhaar — and when not?

Aadhaar is asked for everywhere, but you do not have to hand over a full copy every time. A few sensible habits go a long way:

  • Prefer masked wherever a service will accept it — hotels, coaching centres, local verifications, and many general KYC forms.
  • Share full Aadhaar only when it is genuinely required — for example, where a regulated process needs the complete number and you trust the recipient.
  • Never share your Aadhaar OTP over a call. No legitimate agency asks for your Aadhaar OTP on the phone.
  • Add context to every copy — the purpose and the date — so it cannot be quietly reused somewhere else.

When in doubt, just ask whether a masked version is acceptable. More often than not it is — and you keep control of your full number either way.

Frequently asked questions

Is a masked Aadhaar valid for KYC?

In many cases, yes — a masked Aadhaar showing your last 4 digits is accepted for KYC by numerous banks and services. Some regulated processes still need the full number, so if you are unsure, ask the specific service what they accept before you share.

Does masking change my actual Aadhaar number?

No. Masking only hides digits on the copy you share. Your real Aadhaar number in UIDAI's records stays exactly the same.

Which digits should I hide?
