Need to turn a few photos into a single PDF for a job application, a college form, or a bank submission? You can do it in under a minute right from your phone — no app to install, no sign-up, and nothing uploaded to a stranger's server. Here's how to combine multiple JPG or PNG images into one clean PDF for free.

Why turn photos into a PDF?

Photos are easy to snap, but they are awkward to submit. Most people convert images to PDF for three practical reasons:

  • Single-file uploads. Job portals, scholarship forms, KYC pages, and government applications often ask for "one PDF" of your marksheet, ID, or certificates. If you have three photos, you need them merged into a single document — not three loose attachments.
  • Combining documents. The front and back of an Aadhaar card, a two-page letter, or a stack of receipts belong together. A PDF keeps them in order and treats them as one file.
  • Cleaner sharing. A PDF opens the same way on every phone, laptop, and printer. It will not rotate unexpectedly, get squeezed by a chat app, or land as a messy gallery of images.

Converting also lets you fix orientation and set a deliberate page sequence — which matters when a human on the other end is reviewing your file.

Getting the page order right

Page order is the step people rush — and it is the one reviewers actually notice. Before you convert, glance at the thumbnails and ask whether they read top to bottom the way a person expects: cover page first, then supporting pages, back side after the front.

  • Front before back. For ID cards, put the side with the photo and number first, then the reverse.
  • Follow the printed page numbers. If your document already has page 1, 2, 3, match that exactly.
  • Check orientation. A sideways photo becomes a sideways page. Rotate it upright before converting so nobody has to tilt their screen.

With KaagazPDF you set the order by simply dragging thumbnails, so you can see the finished layout before you commit to a single click.

Controlling the PDF file size

Modern phone cameras produce large images, so a PDF made from several full-resolution photos can easily run into a few megabytes. Many upload forms cap the size — commonly 1 MB, 500 KB, or even less — and will reject anything heavier.

The clean approach is to combine first, then compress. Make your single PDF exactly the way you want it, and if it turns out too heavy, shrink it in one more step.

⚠️ Do not over-compress documents that must stay readable. Squeeze a marksheet or ID too hard and the text or photo can turn blurry, which may get your submission rejected. Reduce just enough to clear the limit.

If your file is above the limit, run it through the compress PDF guide to bring it down to a target size like 100 KB or 50 KB while keeping it legible. Both tools are free and run on your device, so your document never gets uploaded at any stage.

Scan a physical document with your camera

You do not need a dedicated scanner app to digitise paper. Your phone camera plus KaagazPDF does the same job:

  1. Lay the document flat on a plain, contrasting surface in even light — no harsh shadows.
  2. Hold the phone directly above it and frame the page so the edges are straight, then capture. Do the same for each page.
  3. Open the Image to PDF tool, add all the captured photos, and drag them into page order.
  4. Convert to a single PDF, then compress if the file is heavy.

A steady hand and good light do most of the work. Wipe the lens first, avoid glare from overhead bulbs, and keep the whole page inside the frame so nothing gets cut off.

Before converting, drag the photo thumbnails into the sequence you want — that order becomes your page order. If you have already made the PDF and the pages are wrong, just add the images again in the correct order and convert once more. It only takes a few seconds.

My PDF is too large. How do I reduce it?

Combine the photos into one PDF first, then compress that PDF to your target size. See the compress PDF to 50 KB guide for exact steps. You can also start with smaller source images if you plan to reduce photo size in KB beforehand.
