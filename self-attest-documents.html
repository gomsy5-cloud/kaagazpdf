How to Self-Attest Documents in India (for Government, Bank & College Forms)

Updated July 2026 · 6 min read · KaagazPDF Guides

Nearly every admission form, bank KYC packet, passport application and job joining kit in India asks for "self-attested copies" of your documents. The phrase sounds formal, but self-attestation is simply you signing a copy of your own document to confirm it is a true copy of the original. This guide explains what self-attested means, what a correct one looks like, and how to do it on your phone in under a minute — no printer or photocopy shop needed.

What does "self-attested copy" mean?

A self-attested copy is a photocopy or scan of an original document that you have signed yourself to declare it is genuine. Years ago, many copies had to be "attested" by a gazetted officer, a bank manager or a notary who stamped and signed them. To cut down that hassle, a large number of government departments, banks, universities and employers now accept self-attestation — you take responsibility for the copy's authenticity by signing it, instead of running to an officer.

In plain terms: the original stays with you, you submit a copy, and your signature on that copy says, "I certify this is a true copy of my original document." It is a declaration of honesty, so always make the copy from your genuine original.

Why do institutions ask for it?

  • Convenience: You don't have to surrender precious originals like your marksheet or Aadhaar.
  • Accountability: Your signature ties you to the copy and discourages tampering.
  • Speed: Offices can process forms without waiting for third-party attestation.

Common places that ask for self-attested copies include college and university admissions, scholarship forms, bank account opening and KYC, PAN and passport applications, job onboarding, and many government scheme applications. Always read the exact wording on your specific form — some may still want notarised or officer-attested copies for certain documents.

What a proper self-attestation looks like

There is no single fixed format, but a widely accepted convention has settled across most Indian offices. On the photocopy or scan, you usually add three things:

  1. Your signature — the same signature you use on your ID and originals.
  2. The words "Self Attested" (some people write "True copy" or "Attested").
  3. The date, and sometimes your name and phone number below the signature.

These are typically placed in a blank area of the page — often the lower or upper margin — so they don't hide any printed detail. On a photo ID, sign near the photo but not across your face or the text. If your form gives specific instructions (for example, "sign across the photograph" or "attest on the front"), follow those first.

The traditional way: print, sign, scan

The old method still works if you have the equipment:

  1. Photocopy or print the document.
  2. Write "Self Attested" and the date in a margin.
  3. Sign by hand in ink.
  4. Scan or photograph the signed copy to submit it online.

It's reliable, but it needs a printer, a scanner and some time — and if you're applying from your phone at 11 pm the night before a deadline, none of that is handy. That's where doing it digitally helps.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Signing over the printed text or photo: A signature across important details can make them unreadable. Keep it in a margin unless the form says otherwise.
  • Forgetting the date: Many offices treat the date as part of a valid attestation. Add it every time.
  • Illegible or dark copies: A blurry, tilted or too-dark scan may be rejected. Use good light and a flat surface.
  • Attesting only the first page: For multi-page documents, self-attest each page unless told otherwise.
  • Mismatched signature: Use the same signature as on your ID; a wildly different one raises questions.
  • Cropping off edges: Don't cut out document borders, seals or numbers when framing the photo or scan.
⚠️ Only self-attest copies of genuine originals you actually hold. A self-attested copy is a personal declaration that the copy is true, so never attest a document you can't back up with the real thing.

Frequently asked questions

Is a digitally added signature valid for self-attestation?

For self-attestation, most forms simply want your handwritten-style signature on the copy. A drawn signature added on your phone reproduces that the same way a scanned ink signature does. If a particular form insists on physical wet-ink signing or a certified digital signature (DSC), follow that instruction instead.

Do I need to write "Self Attested", or is a signature enough?

A signature alone is often accepted, but adding "Self Attested" with the date is the safer, widely-followed convention and leaves no room for doubt. When unsure, include both.

Can I self-attest without a printer or scanner?

Yes. If you have a photo, scan or PDF of the document, you can add the "Self Attested" text, date and your signature digitally — for example with KaagazPDF, free and on your own device — then download and submit it. No printing required.

Should I sign on every page?

For multi-page documents, it's best to self-attest each page unless the form says only the first page is needed. A tool that lets you apply the attestation to all pages at once saves time.

Is self-attestation the same as notarisation?

No. Notarisation is done by a notary who verifies and stamps the document, and it usually has a fee. Self-attestation is you signing your own copy. Some documents may still require notarised or officer-attested copies, so check what your form asks for.
